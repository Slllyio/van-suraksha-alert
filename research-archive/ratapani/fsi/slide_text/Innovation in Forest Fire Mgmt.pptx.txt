=== Slide 11 ===
Challenges & Issues — Forest Fire Management in Uttarakhand
[image]
[image]
🔥
Traditional and Cultural Practices
Pre-monsoon burning of agricultural residues, grasslands, and waste materials continues to be a significant contributor to forest fire incidents in certain areas.
👥
Alienation of the Community
Declining dependence on forests and perceived restrictions on forest access have, in some areas, reduced commitment of community in forest protection and fire prevention efforts.
🌲
High Fuel Load in Chir Pine Forests
Continuous accumulation of highly inflammable pine needles (Pirul) on forest floors substantially increases fire vulnerability, particularly during the dry season.
⛰️
Difficult Terrain and Accessibility Constraints
Steep slopes, remote locations, and limited accessibility in mountainous regions often impede rapid deployment of firefighting resources.
☁️
Climate Change Impacts
Rising temperatures, prolonged dry spells, erratic precipitation patterns, and increasing frequency of extreme weather events are elevating forest fire risks and extending the fire season.

Fire Alerts by Forest Survey of India (FSI)
Pre-fire alerts are sent by FSI Dehradun to the forest headquarter which is then relayed to field staff through official whatsapp group. The pre-fire alert is sent every week based on weather predictions, forest fire danger rating and other factors.
Weekly weather alert issued by IMD indicating fire danger rating which is then shared with field officers/staff.
During fire season (15th Feb to 30th June) live fire alerts are issued by FSI based on which fire dousing activity is undertaken.
Nodal Officer Forest Fire in the forest headquarters undertakes coordination, monitoring and assessment.
Integrated Control and Command Center (ICCC) in Forest headquarters undertakes forest fire alert status, reporting and follow-up with the Nodal Officer.
Incident Response System at the district level for inter-departmental coordination.
Operational 24X7 setup established at the Integrated Command 
and Control Center (ICCC) in Forest Headquarters

The Ecological Challenges of Chir Pine Forests
15.24 % of Uttarakhand’s total forest area is covered by Chir Pine.
Accumulated ‘Pirul’ (pine needles) on the forest floor acts as a highly combustible carpet. Primary fuel load driving forest fire incidents across the state.

Fire Prevention: Drastic reduction of fuel load in pine-dominated areas to curb fire spread.

=== Slide 30 ===
The Shitlakhet Model: A Community‑led Fire Mitigation Approach
The Shitlakhet Model represents a community-led approach to fire mitigation and forest restoration in the Almora District. 

 Historically, this region was known for its apple orchards, though production ceased after 2005.

 The initiative is driven by local pioneers from the community institutions: Syahidevi Vikas Samiti, Mahila mandal, SHGs, Yuva mangal dals & forest guards.
Location: Syahi Devi Forest/Sacred Grove
(7 km from Shitlakhet), Almora, Uttarakhand.
[image]

=== Slide 31 ===
Historical Context and Fire Protection
Syahi Devi is the oldest forest in the area, becoming a reserved forest in 1877 under the Forest Act of 1865.

 Fire protection in Syahi Devi officially started between 1890 and 1891.

 Despite these early efforts, the reserves suffered disastrous fires over the years

 Historical working plans for the Central Almora Forest Division date back to the 1920s, emphasizing a long-standing need for organized management.

=== Slide 33 ===
The "Golden Hour" Strategy
“The first 60  Minutes determine survival.”

The Challenge: Once a fire in pine forests establishes, it is nearly impossible to stop without heavy machinery.

The Solution: Rapid village mobilization

Tactics: 
 Alert System: WhatsApp groups, early warning system and village alerts, involving forest and revenue departments alongside local volunteers.
 Designated forest guards & gram praharis to monitor the slopes.
 Tools: Villagers rush to sites with basic tools such as sticks, khurpi, water bottles, and headlamps.
 Logistics: The community manages food and water for front-line volunteers.

=== Slide 34 ===
A Replicable Blueprint: Jardhar Gaon, Tehri Garhwal
The Shitlakhet Model shares replicability with other successful community-led initiatives in Jardhar Gaon, Tehri Garhwal, by adopting important lessons:

Recognize the Golden Hour: Decentralize fire response to the village level.

Make ‘Onn Diwas’ a state-wide cultural norm.

Prioritize Assisted Natural Regeneration (ANR) drive over expensive industrial plantations.

=== Slide 38 ===
Consultancy Services under U-PREPARE project
‘Community-based Forest Fire Risk Management and Carbon Finance Programme’:
This assignment seeks to establish an incentive-based community program, including via the provision of community grants for forest fire risk management that will be guided by sustainable forest management principles, reduce fuel load, restore degraded lands, ANR and enhance soil moisture levels. 
Fires release large amounts of stored carbon, worsening climate change - community-based forest fire management can prevent losses and enhance resilience.
Through carbon finance programs  reduced fire risks = more carbon sequestration credits.
Revenues from credits can fund fire prevention, forest restoration and local livelihoods.
‘Training, Capacity Building and Technical Assistance for UKFD’:
This assignment seeks to identify gap, prepare a blueprint document and undertake capacity building & training of the frontline forest staff, key stakeholders and communities to tackle forest fire and to empower the UKFD as the designated ‘First Responder’ for forest fire in sensitive forest areas.
Formulate state level forest fire management strategy document and action plan.
Strengthen community & institutional capacity.
Optimize asset management for rapid response.
