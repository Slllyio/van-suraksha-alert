# Surface fuel load: published numbers and mapping methods

Literature scan, 14 Oct 2026, for Ratapani (tropical dry deciduous, teak dominant). This is a companion to `../fuel_load_tables.md`, which covers the FSI report and deck and is not repeated here. Sources: web search and the Consensus paper index. Every row has a link. Lines marked **[inference]** are my reasoning, not something a paper states.

Units: 1 t/ha = 1 Mg/ha = 100 g/m² = 9.29 g/ft². FSI's "Low" class (< 20 g/ft²) is therefore < 2.15 t/ha.

### 1c. Standard fuel model loads (for comparison and for fire-behaviour runs)

From Scott & Burgan 2005, RMRS-GTR-153, Table 7 ([pdf](https://www.fs.usda.gov/rm/pubs_series/rmrs/gtr/rmrs_gtr153.pdf)). Converted with 1 t/ac = 2.24 t/ha.

| Model | 1-hr | 10-hr | 100-hr | Live herb | Depth | Dead moisture of extinction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TL2 low-load broadleaf litter | 3.14 | 5.16 | 4.93 | 0 | 0.2 ft | 25% |
| TL6 moderate broadleaf litter | 5.38 | 2.69 | 2.69 | 0 | 0.3 ft | 25% |
| TL9 very high broadleaf litter | 14.9 | 7.40 | 9.30 | 0 | 0.6 ft | 35% |
| TU2 litter plus shrub | 2.13 | 4.04 | 2.80 | 0 (live woody 0.45) | 1.0 ft | 30% |
| GR2 moderate grass | 0.22 | 0 | 0 | 2.24 | 1.0 ft | 15% |
| GS1 grass plus shrub | 0.45 | 0 | 0 | 1.12 (live woody 1.46) | 0.9 ft | 15% |

All values are t/ha.

**[inference]** Measured central Indian forest floors are about 2–3.7 t/ha in total. That is close to the TL2 1-hr load alone, but TL2's 10-hr and 100-hr loads (about 10 t/ha) are far heavier than anything measured in central India. A custom TL model is the better fit: TL6 bed depth and SAV, with the 1-hr load set to a local 2–4 t/ha and 10-hr/100-hr set to about 0.5–1 t/ha. Grassy openings would take GR2 or GS1. No Indian paper assigning Scott-Burgan models to deciduous forest turned up.

## 2. What the numbers say for Ratapani

- **Typical value.** Pre-fire forest-floor fuel in central Indian dry deciduous forest is about **2–3.7 t/ha**: Bhoramdeo 2.0–3.65 and Chhattisgarh 2.2–3.38. Teak plantations run heavier, at **5–6.5 t/ha** (Raigarh). **[inference]** That puts most of Ratapani on or just above FSI's Low/Moderate boundary of 2.15 t/ha. The district tables call Raisen 97% "Low", which may understate litter on a teak floor in March.
- **Supply.** Annual leaf fall in MP teak is **2.6–5.0 t/ha/yr** (Chhindwara, Sagar). Monsoon decay clears most of it by October; turnover is 50–92%/yr (AABR 50–62%), and teak decays slowly compared with mixed species.
- **Timing.** Leaf fall starts in December. Monthly peaks fall in January–February (Ranchi: about 1.5 t/ha/month) and in March (Chhindwara). About 75% is shed by late March (Gadchiroli), and fuel builds from February to April. **[inference]** A rough month-by-month curve for a teak stand, as a share of the annual drop: Nov 5%, Dec 10%, Jan 20%, Feb 25%, Mar 25%, Apr 10%. Cumulative floor load rises from about 0.5 t/ha in December to 2.5–4 t/ha in April, minus fire and decay. Calibrate this against Pande 2002 and Toppo 2021 before use.
- **Grass.** In the Western Ghats, grass in open stands adds 0.75–1.8 t/ha over two years. Ratapani's open forest and blanks will behave the same way.
- **Coarse wood.** In the Nilgiris, 1000-hr fuel dominates the total (15 t/ha). It barely affects spread, so it can be ignored for ignition and spread work.

## 3. Mapping methods (10–30 m), each with a "use for us" line

**M1. Stratum mean (FSI, LANDFIRE style).** Assign each class (forest type × density × slope, or EVT × cover × height) a mean load or fuel model from field plots. LANDFIRE FBFM40 uses expert rulesets on existing vegetation type, cover and height, then shifts the model by disturbance type and time since disturbance ([LANDFIRE product page](https://landfire.gov/node/32), [Texas ruleset review](https://morap.missouri.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/Texas_Fire_Modeling_Report_2010.pdf)). Europe and Czechia built crosswalks to Scott-Burgan the same way ([Aragoneses 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/27731375be9052e59293ab8ae09df86c/?utm_source=claude_desktop), [Kudláčková 2026, 30 m](https://consensus.app/papers/details/4010c5afa8bb5c63a0a834b0072b36e1/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
*Use for us:* take the strata we already have and fill them with section 1 values (teak about 5, mixed about 3, open about 2 t/ha, plus grass). Add "time since last burn" from our burn history, as LANDFIRE does. This is the cheapest credible map.

**M2. Random-forest regression on S2 + S1 + terrain.**
- Sichuan, Sentinel-1/2 plus slope: R² 0.59 for total dead fuel, 0.57 for 1-hr (RMSE 0.18 t/ha), but only 0.29 for litter (RMSE 1.66 t/ha) ([Li 2021 IGARSS](https://consensus.app/papers/details/acb1e93f769d5514aeaa6082231e11c3/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
- Germany, lidar plus S2: litter R² 0.27 (dead 1-hr 0.41), only 9% lower RMSE than forest-type means ([Labenski 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d6d64a56d0565da98612bcf98d7b942e/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
- Mexico, UAV multispectral: litter R² 0.32 ([Chávez-Durán 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/4e79f480f1255a648ca91048b0e97141/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
- Active learning lifted 1-hr R² from 0.20 to 0.53 ([Hu 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/264082c0e71353d6b9b4a46501b65a47/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
- Inventory metrics alone reach R² 0.72 for leaf litter ([Herrmann 2026, TLS](https://consensus.app/papers/details/74662f4900b656ec8e65d548cdc085e3/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
*Use for us:* expect litter R² of about 0.3 at best. It is only worth doing with 60 or more of our own plots, and it should be benchmarked against M1 stratum means, as Labenski did.

**M3. Mass balance: litterfall from canopy phenology minus decomposition.** Annual litterfall is estimated from the seasonal drop in foliage biomass (Landsat plus a radiative transfer model), minus weather-driven decay, accumulated over time. Validated on 105 plots: R² 0.67, RMSE 2.56 Mg/ha ([Li et al. 2025, RSE](https://consensus.app/papers/details/4dbe2280760e5e14af684efe459694d4/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). Thakur 2022 also found that litterfall correlates with SAVI.
*Use for us:* **the best fit for a deciduous teak site.** Fuel there is mostly this season's leaf drop, and the canopy loss shows clearly in S2 NDVI/LAI from December to April. Per pixel: load(t) ≈ k × (peak-season LAI − current LAI) + residual from last season, where k comes from the Chhindwara 2.6–4.2 t/ha/yr leaf fall. Reset to zero after a burn. This gives a dynamic fuel layer at 10–20 m. The NDVI leaf-fall proxy for central India ([Singh et al. 2020, Sci. Rep.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7572383/)) is a ready template at 250 m.

**M4. GEDI structure to fuel.** In the Brazilian Cerrado, GEDI with RF predicted total fuel (R² 0.71) and woody fuel (0.88), but surface-stratum fuel only weakly (R² 0.15–0.46) ([Leite 2022, RSE](https://research.bangor.ac.uk/en/publications/large-scale-multi-layer-fuel-load-characterization-in-tropical-sa/)). An ALOS PALSAR plus Landsat fuel paper exists (Li 2021, Remote Sensing); only its title was seen.
*Use for us:* GEDI is useful for canopy cover and height strata (M1 inputs) and for shrub and Lantana layers. It is not a direct litter map.

**M5. Live fuel moisture (LFMC).**
- Globe-LFMC 2.0: 280k samples from 15 countries, **none from India** ([Yebra 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/63d3af64cbd55c6cb1f23f4d624e8c7c/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
- A Sentinel-2 20 m LFMC product exists for Australia: forest R² 0.43, RMSE 21% ([Yebra 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6a8b169e6a3e5616a94144b159dea1f0/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
- Multi-sensor RF/XGBoost (MODIS, VIIRS, Landsat, S2): tree-class R² 0.70 ([Pampanoni 2026](https://consensus.app/papers/details/6add85358f535c0b96d62140b0d21340/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
- S2 optical at 20 m: R² 0.55, and adding S1 did not help ([Tanase 2022](https://consensus.app/papers/details/7c5b70d948335edfa2e5b3f0eed77b72/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
- Northern Thailand deciduous forest, unvalidated S2 NDII/MSI index: deciduous forest dries hard from Jan to Mar ([Chotamonsak 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/659f8ed3be045f11b4aaab6f9168b6a0/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
*Use for us:* in leafless teak, live moisture matters less than dead litter moisture. Use S2 NDII/NDMI as a relative dryness index for the understorey and grass, not as calibrated LFMC.

**M6. Dead fuel moisture (DFMC) from weather.**
- Vapour-pressure-deficit model: fine dead fuel moisture falls exponentially as VPD rises, and VPD can come from MODIS LST or gridded weather ([Nolan 2016, RSE](https://consensus.app/papers/details/89de4c1c63d250ce93b9d989cd4e0336/?utm_source=claude_desktop); applied in Greece with MAE < 2.2% in most fires, [Dragozi 2021](https://consensus.app/papers/details/2f2bc67462a25e5485f3834f9eed1ce3/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
- Geostationary relative humidity plus a fuel-stick model: R² 0.73, RMSE 3.6% ([Fan 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d8a29ae659cf55a38321e9d04fd49416/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
- Fine Fuel Moisture Code was the best predictor of 10-hr stick moisture: R 0.87 ([Kranz 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/7a94143d87ae5502acb719a80b8ff2bf/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
- In-forest VPD plus terrain, with an RF model, for understorey fine DFMC ([Li 2024](https://consensus.app/papers/details/7e350461ae055fea81476e4126d9886a/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).
*Use for us:* compute daily DFMC from ERA5-Land or IMD VPD (Nolan form), adjusted for aspect and slope. The Mudumalai work shows November–December rain controls the fire year, so include it.

**Flammability (no load values).** Teak leaf and wood in Odisha ignite more readily than sal: teak wood moisture 5.1%, volatiles 81.9%, heat value 19.9 MJ/kg ([Behera 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/59f0d7cead0a53e5a2890983ac978072/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).

## 4. Gaps

- No Ratapani or Raisen/Sehore fuel plots were found.
- No ISRO/IIRS fuel-load map at 10–30 m was found; the only ISRO hit was the NRSC forest-type map ([link](https://nices.nrsc.gov.in/docs/forest_type.pdf)).
- No Indian Scott-Burgan crosswalk was found.
- The Bandipur figures are qualitative only (Lantana).
- Saha 2002 g/m² values and Mondal & Sukumar appendix values are now extracted in `fuel_load_values_fullread.md`.

## 5. Top 5 recommendations (ranked)

1. **Build an M3-style dynamic litter layer.** Use S2 canopy loss from Dec to Apr, scaled to 2.6–4.2 t/ha/yr leaf fall (Pande 2002, Chhindwara MP), reset by our burn dates. It is the only method that tracks the January–March build-up that drives Ratapani fires, and it validated at R² 0.67 elsewhere.
2. **Fill our existing strata with literature means now (M1).** Use teak about 5–6.5, mixed dry deciduous about 2–3.7, and open/degraded about 2 t/ha, plus 0.75–1.8 t/ha grass in openings. Use this as the baseline any regression must beat.
3. **Collect a small field set before trying RF regression (M2).** Take about 60–100 one-square-metre plots in late February and early April, stratified by teak/mixed × canopy × burn history. Published litter R² is only about 0.3, so without local plots the stratum means are as good.
4. **Add a daily dead-fuel-moisture layer (M6).** Use the VPD-exponential form from gridded weather, with Nov–Dec rainfall as a season prior. Skip calibrated LFMC: Globe-LFMC has no Indian data and teak is leafless at peak season.
5. **For fire-behaviour runs, use a custom TL fuel model**: a TL6 bed with 1-hr 2–4 t/ha, 10/100-hr about 0.5–1 t/ha, and 25% extinction. Use GR2 or GS1 for grassy blanks. Ask FSI for its central India stratum means to check this. **[inference]**
